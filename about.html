Since 2004 · Houston, Austin, Dallas

A firm built the way
a good structure is built.

Twenty-six lawyers, one standard. We started with eleven client files and a stubborn idea: legal work should look like engineering work — scoped, documented, budgeted, and finished when it is actually done.

Our story

We were tired of losing good cases on bad paperwork

In 2004, Alicia Renteria and Grace Uy had the same complaint from opposite sides of the bar: a contractor with a legitimate delay claim could not prove it, because nobody had told it to keep a contemporaneous record. The firm was founded to close that gap.

The first office was a shared suite on Franklin Street with two lawyers, one paralegal and a rules compliance calendar printed and taped to the wall. Twenty-two years later the calendar is still there — it is now laminated, and it is still the most-used object in the firm.

We grew slowly and on purpose. No branch in a city we cannot drive to for a hearing. No practice group that cannot staff its own trial. No lateral hire without a writing sample and a mock argument. That discipline is the reason a client can have a partner on the phone in one hour, and the reason we can tell you what a matter will cost before it starts.

“The best argument in the world does not help if the notice was late. So we teach notice first and argument second.”

Alicia D. Renteria · Founding & Managing Partner

Twenty-two years

How the firm got here

A short history of the milestones that changed how we practise, not just how big we were.

2004

Two litigators, one conference room

Alicia Renteria and Grace Uy leave a Houston insurance-defence shop with eleven client files and a shared conviction: most construction cases are lost on documents, not on law.

2009

The recession book of work

Foreclosure defence, mechanic's lien priority and contractor insolvency fill the docket. The firm's payment-security practice is built in a courthouse basement, one deadline at a time.

2013

Austin office opens

Three lawyers move to a shared suite near the Capitol for entitlement and public-procurement work. The first associate class is hired the same spring.

2017

An in-house engineering team

Two former project engineers join as claims analysts. Schedule analysis and cost modelling come in-house, and outside expert spend drops by a third.

2021

Fixed-fee standard published

Every scoping engagement gets a written price before work starts. Clients begin comparing us on cost as well as outcomes — which is the point.

2024

Twenty years, twenty-six lawyers

Dallas office opens. The firm caps headcount growth at eight lawyers a year so that partner-to-associate staffing stays above one to two.

2026

A practice for what comes next

New groups for data and proptech contracts, and a standing pro bono commitment of 40 hours per lawyer per year for displaced workers.

Principles

Four rules we actually follow

Every one of these has cost the firm money at least once. That is how we know they are real.

Candour over comfort

We tell clients what a matter is worth and what it will cost, in writing, before they need a second opinion on our advice. Two of our best years came from cases we talked people out of.

Documents decide

Notice clauses, daily reports, RFIs, minutes, timesheets. Our first invoice is almost always for evidence work, because evidence is what settles cases.

One partner, start to finish

The lawyer who takes your call argues the motion. Partner-to-associate ratios in our practice groups are published in the engagement letter.

Deadlines are sacred

Lien, bond and appeal windows do not extend because a matter was complicated. Every file runs on a dual-calendared, independently audited deadline sheet.

Community

Pro bono, teaching and the trades

Forty hours per lawyer per year, tracked in the same system as billable work and reported to the whole firm each quarter.

  • Small contractor clinic. Monthly Saturday intake with the Houston Bar Association: lien deadlines, subcontract review, and how to answer a demand letter.
  • Trades education. Our construction group teaches a notice-and-records module in two community-college construction-management programmes.
  • Displaced workers. Standing panel for unpaid-wage claims after employer insolvency, with three local legal-aid organisations.
  • Housing court clinic. Volunteer nights representing tenants in Harris County justice courts, six times a year.
