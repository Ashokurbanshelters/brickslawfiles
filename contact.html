Before you call

Questions clients actually ask intake

If your question is not here, it is a good question for the first call.

01Does the 30-minute consultation really cost nothing?

Yes, for prospective clients with a matter we handle. It is a genuine scoping call: you describe the facts, we tell you whether there is a claim, what the deadlines look like, and roughly what the work costs. If we think you do not need a lawyer yet, we will say that on the call.

02Will you take my case if you already act for someone on this project?

Usually not — conflicts are the reason we ask for the adverse party's name in the form. If we have an unrelated engagement with that entity, we may be able to act with informed written consent from both sides, subject to the Texas Disciplinary Rules. Either way you get an answer the same day, and if we cannot act we will suggest two firms that can.

03How fast can you file something if a deadline is imminent?

We keep a filing package template and a courier relationship for Harris County, Dallas and Travis counties. A TRO or a mechanics-lien affidavit can be drafted, signed and filed within 24 hours of engagement, and often faster if the documents already exist. Call the main line and say “deadline”; it jumps the queue.

04What should I have ready for the first call?

The contract and any subcontracts in the chain, the notice or demand letter you received, the schedule (native file if you have it), the daily reports or logs covering the period, and your own chronology of key dates. Even a messy folder is useful — do not spend three weeks tidying it first.

05Do you work on contingency or flat fees?

Both, selectively. Roughly 40% of engagements carry a fixed fee for a defined scope (contract review, diligence, policy audits, assessment memos). Contingency is available for strong payment claims against solvent defendants, and hybrid structures where a client needs downside protection. We will tell you which one we recommend and why.

06Who actually does the work — a partner or an associate?

A named partner owns the strategy, drafts or heavily marks up the important documents, and appears at hearings that matter. Associates and paralegals do the discovery, research, chronology and construction work, and their hours are itemised at tenth-of-an-hour granularity. The engagement letter states the staffing ratio for your matter group.

07Do you take matters outside Texas?

Yes, where we can associate competent local counsel or the forum does not require admission — arbitrations, mediations and document-only work are common. We decline to litigate in jurisdictions where we cannot offer local procedural judgment, and we will say so rather than learn it on your file.

08How do I get my file when the matter ends?

Every closing letter includes a download link with the full work product, executed documents and correspondence, available for 24 months. We retain the original file for ten years under Texas guidance and will release it to you or your new counsel at any time, at no charge beyond copying costs.

Answers are general information about how the firm operates, not legal advice. Texas-specific deadlines vary by project type and contract; confirm yours with a lawyer.
