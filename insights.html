Reading the room at mediation: when to trade certainty for speed

Three variables decide it: how much of the file the other side has already seen, who pays the decision-maker’s salary, and whether your own client can absorb a bad number faster than a good delay. Overlay districts and the 2026 affordability set-aside: a developer’s checklist

Staff reviewers now expect three things before a rezoning reaches the dais: an affordability or set-aside memo, a traffic and drainage package, and evidence of neighbourhood engagement dated before the application. Buy-sell agreements that survive a founder’s exit

A buy-sell survives when three things are settled in advance: the valuation method (formula, appraisal or bid), who funds the purchase and over what term, and the clock on deadlock. Silence on any one of them converts a disagreement into litigation with a friendly forum and a hostile timeline.

Wage-and-hour audit season: five records Texas employers cannot produce

The five records employers most often cannot produce: contemporaneous time records for crew leads, travel-time capture between sites, tool and per-diem treatment, unpaid training or pre-shift meetings, and off-clock messaging. Each is documentary, each is cheap to fix prospectively, and each has decided a collective action.

Private client14 Aug 2025Briefing note

When the business is the estate plan: a note for owner-operators

For an owner-operator the estate plan is only real if it survives the guaranties. Test who can sign, who must consent, what the lender can call, whether key-person cover matches replacement cost, and whether the trust actually owns the membership interests. A document that leaves the company unable to bid is not a plan.
